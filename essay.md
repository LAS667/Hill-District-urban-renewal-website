	In my Pennsylvania Public School education, we were told that racism & segregation ended in the 60s with the Civil Rights movement. But it became clear to me as I was growing up in & around Philadelphia 50 years from that time, that this is far from true, especially in the built environment. It was clear to me that access to healthy food, well-maintained housing, and economic opportunity seemed disproportionately available to white people, and not available to other groups.

	In Pittsburgh, things are very similar, if not more apparent than in Philly. In the 60s, places like Homewood, East Liberty, and The Hill District were majority immigrant, Jewish, & African American neighborhoods bustling with community & opportunity. But since then, these places have been suffering from urban decay, disinvestment, and neoliberal gentrification. In East Liberty, developers preserve historical buildings as a façade of neighborhood identity whilst Whole Foods, Target, Google & more buy these places up, or get built in the place of previous low-income apartments often populated by mostly black residents. If racism & segregation ended in the 60s, then why does this structural inequality still exist nearly 60 years later in Pittsburgh?
